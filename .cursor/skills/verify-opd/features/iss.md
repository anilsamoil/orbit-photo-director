# ISS view

The tab immediately after Map opens a modeled view from the station. Horizon is the camera when no aim is stored. Straight down is the other camera. The choice stays for this page. A reload in the same tab restores it from sessionStorage `opd-iss-aim`. A new browser session restores it from localStorage `opd-iss-aim`. A link whose hash carries `iss` restores that aim when the tab has no sessionStorage aim. `r` clears all three. Escape clears all three when `#profile-menu` is closed, and only closes that menu when it is open. The pane shows a UTC clock, four text lines under it from that same instant, a compact ISS perspective card, and a Details disclosure. Leaving the tab disposes the scene. Map, Queue, Upcoming, Profile, and Log stay where they were. The map Time strip does not drive this clock.

## Sub-features

- `iss-tab` is `#tab-iss`, labeled `ISS view`, directly after `#tab-map`.
- `iss-horizon` presses `[data-iss-preset="horizon"]` and the card says `Horizon locked`.
- `iss-nadir` presses `[data-iss-preset="nadir"]`, labeled `Straight down`, and the card says `Nadir locked`.
- `iss-card` starts collapsed. The toggle is `Telemetry`, `aria-expanded` is `false`, and the body is hidden. Opening it keeps the card off the Earth frame. The card shows `14 mm` and the cloud-free Blue Marble and Black Marble 2016 line. The UTC clock is on the toolbar, not in the card.
- `iss-clock` is `[data-iss-clock]` on the toolbar. The children, in order, are `[data-iss-utc]`, `[data-iss-gmt-day]`, `[data-iss-houston]`, `[data-iss-day-month]`, and `[data-iss-weekday]`. The GMT day sits directly under the UTC line at the UTC font size. The two lines together are within 1px of 44px, the height of the toolbar buttons. Houston, the date, and the weekday stay at 0.68rem. The UTC line is `HH:MM:SS UTC` from the card's position stamp. Houston is `America/Chicago` as `HH:MM:SS` with `CDT` or `CST`. The GMT day is `GMT` plus three digits and no space, such as `GMT276`. The date line is the GMT day number and a short month, such as `4 oct`. The last line is the full weekday name, such as `Saturday`. All five are that one instant. `issClockLines` in `frontend/src/iss-view/model.ts` formats the four lines. `writeCard` in `frontend/src/iss-view/index.ts` paints them from the same `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS UTC` prefix. A paint rewrites them together. That paint is the half-second timer and any aim, including Straight down. The map Time strip is a different control and does not call `nowMs`. The clock block is `pointer-events: none`. The four lines are paragraphs with `min-height: 0` and a line box shorter than 44px. They are not controls. An unavailable orbit sets the card position to a sentence that starts `Orbit unavailable`, the UTC prefix does not match, and all five texts are cleared. Leaving the tab removes the block.
- `iss-edition` is the paragraph `[data-iss-edition]` reading `Expedition 75 Beta Edition` at the UTC font size. It is not inside `[data-iss-frame]`. On a window wider than 720px it starts under the toolbar `?` (`[data-iss-aim-help]`, Keyboard shortcuts), below the button row and inside the toolbar, and covers no button. It is hidden while the keyboard sheet is open and shows again when the sheet closes. At 720px and below the toolbar is a column and that paragraph is the last row.
- `iss-launches` is a native `[data-iss-launch-picker]` in `[data-iss-controls]`, beside Telemetry. It starts at `Choose launch`. Ungrouped options are the map's chances for the next seven days, each with its site and the UTC time. An `All launches` group lists every launch in the next 14 days whose status is Go, Confirmed, or TBC and whose precision is Second, Minute, or Hour, including rows whose assessment is unknown, no chance, or too far. `Verify Horizon` is that group on the verify fixture. Choosing it shows the pad pin or edge arrow and the card, sets `data-iss-launch-group` to `all`, leaves `data-iss-launch-corridor` off, and the card does not say a chance. A click on `.iss-launch-pin` or `[data-iss-launch-edge]` aims through `onLaunchLook` in `frontend/src/iss-view/index.ts`. When the group is `all`, that callback reads the All launches list. Chance rows keep the ungrouped options. `None` clears the choice. Choosing one keeps the camera aim and shows one `[data-iss-launch]` pad for that site. The label is `Look toward` and the site name. `[data-iss-launch-arrow]` rotates in the rolled frame, ahead pointing down. A press aims about 18° toward that site. A site on the disk and outside the frame is `[data-iss-launch-edge]`. A site below the limb has the pad arrow and neither mark. `[data-iss-launch-card]` names the launch, the site, the launch window or tentative NET, and the current visibility (`Site in frame`, `Site outside frame`, `Site below horizon`, or `View unavailable`). On a wide pane the card sits beside the frame. On a narrow pane it sits under the frame, unless the below-card's own height would leave the earth's short side under 120px. That height is measured with the below layout applied, so the overlay's cap cannot feed the next fit. Under 120px the card enters `over`. From `over` it returns under the earth only when that short side is 132px or more. 120 through 131 keeps the current place. A side card whose reserved short side is under 80px uses `over` as well. On that overlay the card sits on the lower edge. The name and the visibility line stay pinned. Only the launch window scrolls. Those floors apply to the reserved layout. `over` keeps the card-closed earth and does not grow it. `placeLaunchMarks` in `frontend/src/iss-view/launches.ts` draws neither a pin nor an arrow when either side is under `LAUNCH_MARK_MIN_PX` (80), so an on-disk site then reads `View unavailable`. A site below the limb still reads `Site below horizon` and has neither mark, because that sentence is chosen before the frame size. Known product gap: iPhone 13 window inner height 565 and below, with the pad on the disk. At 565 the earth is 109 by 72. At 548, 556, and 564 the earth is 108 by 72. `data-iss-launch-place` is `over` and the card reads `View unavailable`. At inner height 580 the earth is 131 by 87 and the card reads `Site outside frame`. This drive does not open those heights, and a frame under 80px on a pane it does open still fails. The 390 by 565 pane below is the scene box, not this window height. `[data-iss-frame]` sets `data-iss-launch-corridor` to `on` only for the selected launch when that artifact already has a sourced track. The choice is not saved. A reload returns the menu to `Choose launch`. A paint does not rebuild the open menu's option or clear the choice. When the chosen launch leaves a judged chance list, the menu returns to `Choose launch` and `[data-iss-launch-card]` keeps `Selected launch is no longer available` until a new choice or `None`. The launch coming back does not select it again. An unsettled catalog, including a newer pointer that is still downloading, does not clear the choice. Source also gives `.iss-launch-pin` a 44px hit area. The painted-open menu across a tick and the 44px pin are not live-forced. The unavailable sentence is. A newer pointer whose body is still downloading is live-forced: the menu stays on the selected launch until that body arrives.
- `iss-credits` is one `.maplibregl-ctrl-attrib-button` inside the scene, without `maplibregl-compact-show`. The credit text is `NASA Blue Marble Next Generation and VIIRS Black Marble 2016`. Natural Earth place names do not add a line.
- `iss-labels` sets `data-iss-place-layers` to `country city town region water` on the frame. The names are `.iss-place` markers. At the 14 mm field the markers are the Natural Earth 110m country and sea shapes in `GROUND_SHAPES` plus the hand cities in `PLACE_CITIES`, which have no rank and no `maxFovDeg`. `label-catalog.json` holds cities, admin-1 regions, and water (gulfs, seas, lakes, and rivers). Vite emits that file as a hashed asset. `requestNear()` loads it with `fetchCatalog` (`cache: 'no-store'`) when the renderer is created and again on every aim, so the first paint does not wait on it. A catalog point is drawn when the field is at or under that point's `maxFovDeg`. `label-catalog-towns.json` holds towns and is emitted the same way. `requestTowns()` fetches it only when the vertical field is at or under `TOWN_LABEL_FOV_DEG` (18). A non-OK status, a JSON parse failure, or an abort rejects that attempt. `pullChunk` then waits `min(16, 2^(fails-1))` ticks of the 500 ms paint and fetches again. Cities go through `placeScreenLabels`: a hand city (`rank` undefined) first, then population rank, then `maxFovDeg`, then name. Country, region, water, and town names use the center-distance score.
- `iss-sides` puts `Starboard` on the left of the frame and `Port` on the right. The Earth camera is rolled 180°, so ground-track forward is at the bottom and port stays on the station's port side.
- `iss-cupola` is `[data-iss-cupola]`. Window 7 is `Window 7 · Nadir` and selects Straight down. Windows 1 through 6 aim the limb. Window 1 is port, 2 forward port, 3 forward starboard, 4 starboard, 5 aft starboard, 6 aft port. Forward bisects 2 and 3. Choosing one clears pan and sets the optical field back to the 14 mm lens, 81.2° vertical. `[data-iss-window]` then reads `W1` through `W7` with the plate name on its label. Horizon and Straight down hide that chip. `r` returns to Horizon and hides the chip.
- `iss-pan` is a drag on `[data-iss-frame]`. It shifts the boresight in the rolled view. Altitude stays on the ephemeris. The optical field of view stays where pinch or wheel left it. A later orbit tick does not snap the aim back to the preset boresight. Horizon and Straight down clear the offset and leave the pinched field. A Cupola window clears the offset and restores the lens field. The offset eases toward a limit instead of hitting a wall. From straight down, a wider field allows less turn, because that frame reaches the limb sooner. Past a horizon aim, a narrower field allows less extra turn into space. A drag back off that limit moves the Earth immediately.
- `iss-hint` is one line on the Earth frame, `[data-iss-hint]`, reading `Pinch or scroll the field`. It does not sit in the toolbar. Horizon's title is `Horizon aim`. Straight down's title is `Aim straight down`. The Cupola select's title is `Window field of view`.
- `iss-key-help` is `[data-iss-aim-help]`, a `?` button on the leading edge of the toolbar, labeled `Keyboard shortcuts`. Tap opens `[data-iss-aim-sheet]`. The rows read Arrows Pan, W/A/S/D Pan, Shift+arrows Fine pan, Shift+W/A/S/D Fine pan, `+ / =` Narrow FOV, `-` Widen, `r / Esc` Reset, `1–7` Cupola, `h` Horizon, and `n` Straight down. `n` is Straight down. `s` pans down. Tap the button again, or tap `[data-iss-aim-scrim]`, to close. While the sheet is open, arrow, field, preset, window, and `r` keys do not aim. Escape closes the sheet and does not reset. A focused Cupola select still keeps its keys. The corner `#help-fab` still opens SNAP help. Opening the sheet does not write storage.
- `iss-plan-inset` is the button `[data-pip="plan"]`, labeled `Show the map`. It is a 148 by 96 mercator preview of the ISS position and the current ground track. The viewport must be at least 800px wide and 600px tall. It is on iPad Pro 11 portrait and landscape, at 1194 by 710 and 1194 by 700, and on desktop at 1280 by 800 and 1280 by 700. It is absent on iPhone 13 and iPhone 17 Pro, in portrait and at 874 by 402, 844 by 390, and 932 by 430, and the scene padding on those phones stays the padding they have today. On a fitting viewport the scene keeps `padding-bottom: 7.75rem` while it is not fullscreen, and the inset sits 12px from the left and bottom of `#iss-pane`, under Telemetry and off Port, Starboard, the clock, and the launch card. `data-iss-fullscreen-active` hides it. Exit shows it again. A mouse click on desktop, or `touchscreen.tap` on iPad, switches to Map and moves focus to `#tab-map`.
- `iss-fullscreen` is `[data-iss-fullscreen]`, a 44px button directly after `[data-iss-aim-anchor]`, labeled `Full screen`. The toolbar `?` lives in that anchor. The modes are `off`, `overlay`, and `element`. A press from `off` sets `data-iss-fullscreen-active` on `[data-iss-scene]`, relabels the button `Exit full screen`, and requests fullscreen. `requestFullscreen` is tried first. `webkitRequestFullscreen` is used only when the standard method is missing. A grant on `fullscreenchange` or `webkitfullscreenchange` moves the mode to `element`. With neither method, or when the grant is refused, the mode stays `overlay` and the scene covers the viewport by CSS. The product does not branch on iPhone. Only the Earth canvas, `Port`, `Starboard`, the clock, and the exit button stay. `.iss-place` markers, the hint, the field readout, the attribution (`.maplibregl-control-container`, which holds `.maplibregl-ctrl-attrib`), and the card go. The error phase keeps the card. The launch corridor and the pad leave the canvas (`showLaunches([])`). A chance pick held while the catalog is not judged, including an offline schedule, takes that same hide inside the early return in `syncLaunchChrome`: fullscreen hides the corridor and the pad, the menu stays on the launch, and leaving fullscreen draws that site again. That offline hide is in `frontend/test/iss-view-fullscreen.test.ts` and is not live-forced. An open shortcut sheet closes through a click on `[data-iss-aim-scrim]` when fullscreen starts. While `[data-iss-aim-open]` is set, the toolbar is `z-index: 6` with `pointer-events: none`, the scrim is `z-index: 5`, and this button keeps `pointer-events: auto` at `z-index: 1`. The button again, Escape, the browser's own exit, or focus outside the scene ends it. That Escape does not reset the aim, and neither does its key repeat. An open dialog takes Escape first. The frame follows the scene box whenever its size changes. A device pixel ratio change redraws the canvas at that ratio, capped at 1.5, on the next paint.
- `iss-keys` aims while the ISS view is focused and the phase is running. Arrow keys nudge the boresight by 4% of the field and ease off the same way a drag does. `w`, `a`, `s`, and `d` take that same nudge for up, left, down, and right. Shift held with an arrow, or with `w`, `a`, `s`, or `d`, uses that same nudge at a quarter of the step. `+` and `=` narrow the optical field. `-` widens it. `r` returns to Horizon, restores the lens field, and deletes `opd-iss-aim` from sessionStorage and localStorage and drops `#iss=`. Escape does the same when the profile menu is closed. While `#profile-menu` is open, Escape closes that menu and leaves the aim, the camera, and the view mode where they are. Digit keys `1` through `7` select Cupola windows W1 through W7 the same way the select does. Window 7 selects Straight down and restores the 14 mm lens. `h` and `H` select Horizon the same way the Horizon button does. `n` and `N` select Straight down the same way that button does. Those preset keys clear pan, leave a pinched field in place, hide the window chip, and write `opd-iss-aim` with no window. A focused input, textarea, select, or contenteditable keeps the key. Cmd and Ctrl chords do too. An open dialog keeps Escape, and it keeps `h` and `n` as well.
- `iss-fov-readout` is `[data-iss-fov]` on the frame. It shows the optical field in degrees, one decimal, for as long as the ISS tab is open. `data-iss-fov-state` stays `live`. Pinch, wheel, `+`, `=`, and `-` rewrite that text from the same field. `r` and a Cupola window rewrite it when they restore the 14 mm lens. Leaving the tab removes the node with the scene.
- `iss-leave` returns to Map and Queue with no `[data-iss-scene]` left mounted. Opening ISS view again keeps Straight down, the last Cupola window, any pan offset, and the pinched field of view for the page. A reload of the same tab restores the window, the pan, and that field from sessionStorage `opd-iss-aim`. A new browser session restores that aim from localStorage `opd-iss-aim`. A link with `#iss=` restores it when the tab has no sessionStorage aim. `r` clears all three, so the next reload starts on Horizon with the lens field.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Open the app on Map. Tap `ISS view` in the top bar.
- The Earth limb is the first picture. Pinch or scroll to narrow the field, then drag the Earth to aim it. On a desktop keyboard, with the view focused, the arrow keys nudge that aim, `w`, `a`, `s`, and `d` nudge up, left, down, and right the same way, Shift with an arrow or with `w`, `a`, `s`, or `d` nudges a quarter of that step, `+` or `=` narrows the field, `-` widens it, `r` resets, Escape resets when the profile menu is closed, `1` through `7` select Cupola windows W1 through W7, `h` selects Horizon, and `n` selects Straight down. Tap `?` on the toolbar to read that list. Tap it again, or tap outside the list, to close it. While the list is open those keys do not aim, and Escape closes the list. A hard drag eases off while Earth stays in the frame. Tap `Straight down` for the ground under the station, or pick a Cupola window. The toolbar shows `W3` for window 3, and clears that mark for Horizon or Straight down. A window, Horizon, or Straight down clears the drag. A window also widens a pinched field back to the 14 mm lens. Horizon and Straight down leave that pinch. Reload the page and open ISS view again: the same window, pan, and field are back. `r`, then reload: Horizon and the lens field, and `opd-iss-aim` is gone from sessionStorage, localStorage, and the `#iss=` hash. A new browser session brings the last aim back from localStorage. A link with `#iss=` brings that aim back when this tab has no sessionStorage aim.
- Tap the corner-arrows button beside `?` to fill the screen with the Earth, the side labels, and the clock. Tap it again, or press Escape, to come back. That Escape does not reset the aim.
- Tap `Map` or `Queue` to leave. Tap `ISS view` again to come back with the same camera, the same `W#` when a window was selected, the same pan, and the same pinched field. The frame line says `Pinch or scroll the field`. The frame also shows the field in degrees the whole time the tab is open. A pinch, a scroll, or `+` / `=` / `-` updates that number. Under the UTC clock, the GMT day, Houston time, the day and month, and the weekday stay with that second. The GMT day is as large as the UTC time. They are text, not buttons. If the orbit cannot be drawn, those four lines and the UTC line go blank.

## Gotchas

- The picture is a spherical model with static Blue Marble and Black Marble 2016. It is not a live photo and not the Cupola window. `clampOpticalFov` floors the optical field at `MIN_OPTICAL_FOV_DEG` (2°), including a stored aim under 2°. `map.setVerticalFieldOfView` follows that field. The credit line stays the NASA Blue Marble and Black Marble 2016 sentence.
- WebKit in this harness may not draw the globe. Chrome is the visual proof of the labels and the zoom. WebKit still has to lay the pane out and report no page errors.
- A failed towns fetch recovers without a reload. `fetchCatalog` in `frontend/src/iss-view/catalog-load.ts` uses `cache: 'no-store'`. The service worker precaches both hashed JSON files. `frontend/test/iss-catalog-retry.test.ts` covers a second request after a 500 and after a 404.
