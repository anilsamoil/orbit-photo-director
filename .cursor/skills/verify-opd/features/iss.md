# ISS view

The tab immediately after Map opens a modeled view from the station. Horizon is the camera on first open of a browser session. Straight down is the other camera. The choice stays for this page, and a reload in the same browser session restores it from sessionStorage. The pane shows UTC, a compact ISS perspective card, and a Details disclosure. Leaving the tab disposes the scene. Map, Queue, Upcoming, Profile, and Log stay where they were.

## Sub-features

- `iss-tab` is `#tab-iss`, labeled `ISS view`, directly after `#tab-map`.
- `iss-horizon` presses `[data-iss-preset="horizon"]` and the card says `Horizon locked`.
- `iss-nadir` presses `[data-iss-preset="nadir"]`, labeled `Straight down`, and the card says `Nadir locked`.
- `iss-card` starts collapsed. The toggle is `Telemetry`, `aria-expanded` is `false`, and the body is hidden. Opening it keeps the card off the Earth frame. The card shows `14 mm`, the cloud-free Blue Marble and Black Marble 2016 line, and a UTC clock.
- `iss-credits` is one `.maplibregl-ctrl-attrib-button` inside the scene, without `maplibregl-compact-show`.
- `iss-labels` sets `data-iss-place-layers` to `country city water` on the frame. The names are markers on the globe.
- `iss-sides` puts `Starboard` on the left of the frame and `Port` on the right. The Earth camera is rolled 180°, so ground-track forward is at the bottom and port stays on the station's port side.
- `iss-cupola` is `[data-iss-cupola]`. Window 7 is `Window 7 · Nadir` and selects Straight down. Windows 1 through 6 aim the limb. Window 1 is port, 2 forward port, 3 forward starboard, 4 starboard, 5 aft starboard, 6 aft port. Forward bisects 2 and 3. Choosing one clears pan and sets the optical field back to the 14 mm lens, 81.2° vertical. `[data-iss-window]` then reads `W1` through `W7` with the plate name on its label. Horizon and Straight down hide that chip. Reset returns to Horizon and hides the chip.
- `iss-pan` is a drag on `[data-iss-frame]`. It shifts the boresight in the rolled view. Altitude stays on the ephemeris. The optical field of view stays where pinch or wheel left it. A later orbit tick does not snap the aim back to the preset boresight. Horizon and Straight down clear the offset and leave the pinched field. A Cupola window clears the offset and restores the lens field. The offset eases toward a limit instead of hitting a wall. From straight down, a wider field allows less turn, because that frame reaches the limb sooner. Past a horizon aim, a narrower field allows less extra turn into space. A drag back off that limit moves the Earth immediately.
- `iss-reset` is `[data-iss-reset]`, labeled `Reset`, beside the aim controls. The pill also shows `· double-tap`. Its title and accessible name say a double-tap does the same. Reset and a double-tap on `[data-iss-frame]` return to Horizon, clear pan, restore the 81.2° lens field, and remove sessionStorage `opd-iss-aim`. Horizon and Straight down do not restore that lens field.
- `iss-hint` is one line on the Earth frame, `[data-iss-hint]`, reading `Pinch or scroll the field · double-tap to reset`. It does not sit in the toolbar. Horizon's title is `Horizon aim`. Straight down's title is `Aim straight down`. The Cupola select's title is `Window field of view`.
- `iss-key-help` is `[data-iss-aim-help]`, a `?` button on the leading edge of the toolbar, labeled `Keyboard shortcuts`. Tap opens `[data-iss-aim-sheet]`. The rows read Arrows Pan, W/A/D Pan, Shift+arrows Fine pan, Shift+W/A/D Fine pan, `+ / =` Narrow FOV, `-` Widen, `r / Esc` Reset, `1–7` Cupola, `h` Horizon, and `s / S` Straight down. `s / S` is Straight down, not pan down. Tap the button again, or tap `[data-iss-aim-scrim]`, to close. While the sheet is open, arrow, field, preset, window, and `r` keys do not aim. Escape closes the sheet and does not reset. A focused Cupola select still keeps its keys. The corner `#help-fab` still opens SNAP help. Opening the sheet does not write storage.
- `iss-keys` aims while the ISS view is focused and the phase is running. Arrow keys nudge the boresight by 4% of the field and ease off the same way a drag does. `w`, `a`, and `d` take that same nudge for up, left, and right. Shift held with an arrow, or with `w`, `a`, or `d`, uses that same nudge at a quarter of the step. Down pan stays on ArrowDown and Shift+ArrowDown. `s` and `S` stay Straight down and do not pan down. `+` and `=` narrow the optical field. `-` widens it. `r` and Escape do what Reset does, including deleting `opd-iss-aim`. Digit keys `1` through `7` select Cupola windows W1 through W7 the same way the select does. Window 7 selects Straight down and restores the 14 mm lens. `h` and `H` select Horizon the same way the Horizon button does. `s` and `S` select Straight down the same way that button does. Those preset keys clear pan, leave a pinched field in place, hide the window chip, and write `opd-iss-aim` with no window. A focused input, textarea, select, or contenteditable keeps the key. Cmd and Ctrl chords do too. An open dialog keeps Escape, and it keeps `h` and `s` as well.
- `iss-fov-readout` is `[data-iss-fov]` on the frame. It shows the optical field in degrees, one decimal, for as long as the ISS tab is open. `data-iss-fov-state` stays `live`. Pinch, wheel, `+`, `=`, and `-` rewrite that text from the same field. Reset and a Cupola window rewrite it when they restore the 14 mm lens. Leaving the tab removes the node with the scene.
- `iss-leave` returns to Map and Queue with no `[data-iss-scene]` left mounted. Opening ISS view again keeps Straight down, the last Cupola window, any pan offset, and the pinched field of view for the page. A reload of the same tab restores the window, the pan, and that field from sessionStorage `opd-iss-aim`. A new browser session, or Reset followed by a reload, starts on Horizon with the lens field.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Open the app on Map. Tap `ISS view` in the top bar.
- The Earth limb is the first picture. Pinch or scroll to narrow the field, then drag the Earth to aim it. On a desktop keyboard, with the view focused, the arrow keys nudge that aim, `w`, `a`, and `d` nudge up, left, and right the same way, Shift with an arrow or with `w`, `a`, or `d` nudges a quarter of that step, `+` or `=` narrows the field, `-` widens it, `r` or Escape resets, `1` through `7` select Cupola windows W1 through W7, `h` selects Horizon, and `s` selects Straight down rather than panning down. Tap `?` on the toolbar to read that list. Tap it again, or tap outside the list, to close it. While the list is open those keys do not aim, and Escape closes the list. A hard drag eases off while Earth stays in the frame. Tap `Straight down` for the ground under the station, or pick a Cupola window. The toolbar shows `W3` for window 3, and clears that mark for Horizon or Straight down. A window, Horizon, or Straight down clears the drag. A window also widens a pinched field back to the 14 mm lens. Horizon and Straight down leave that pinch. `Reset · double-tap`, or a double-tap on the Earth, returns to Horizon, clears the drag, and restores that lens. Reload the page and open ISS view again: the same window, pan, and field are back. Reset, then reload: Horizon and the lens field, and `opd-iss-aim` is gone.
- Tap `Map` or `Queue` to leave. Tap `ISS view` again to come back with the same camera, the same `W#` when a window was selected, the same pan, and the same pinched field. The frame line says `Pinch or scroll the field · double-tap to reset`. The frame also shows the field in degrees the whole time the tab is open. A pinch, a scroll, or `+` / `=` / `-` updates that number.

## Gotchas

- The picture is a spherical model with static Blue Marble and Black Marble 2016. It is not a live photo and not the Cupola window.
- The scene needs WebGL2. A browser without it shows `WebGL2 is unavailable` and this drive fails on the Horizon wait.
- The preset, a pan offset, and a pinched field stay for this page session. Leaving ISS view and opening it again keeps that offset and that field. A reload in the same browser session reads sessionStorage `opd-iss-aim` before the first paint and restores the mode, the Cupola window, the pan, and the optical field. A new browser session has no key and starts on Horizon with the 14 mm lens. Horizon, Straight down, and a Cupola window clear the pan. A Cupola window restores the 81.2° lens. Horizon and Straight down leave a pinch in place. `Reset` and a double-tap return to Horizon and that lens and delete the key, so the next reload stays on Horizon. Pinch and wheel write the field into the in-memory preset and into `opd-iss-aim`, so a tab return matches a reload. Arrow keys, `w`, `a`, `d`, Shift with those keys, `+`, `=`, and `-` write that same key. Digit keys `1` through `7` write it the same way the Cupola select does. `h` and `s` write it the same way the Horizon and Straight down buttons do, with no window and with the pinched field left in place. `r` and Escape delete it, the same as Reset.
- Pan shifts the boresight in the rolled view and leaves altitude on the ephemeris. The next orbit tick keeps that offset. It does not freeze the station.
- The fixture uses a live CelesTrak ISS element set when that fetch works. While that set is not over 48 hours old, the open card's first line is `ISS perspective`. If CelesTrak is unreachable, the fixture uses the 2026-09-27 set in `scripts/fixtures.mjs`, and that line is `Estimated view · orbit data old`. The drive still requires `Horizon locked` and `14 mm`.
- G1 is not closed by this drive. Emulated WebKit is not a physical iPad Safari or iPhone Safari pass.
