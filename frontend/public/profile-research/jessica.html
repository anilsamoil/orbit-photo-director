Jessica Meir photography targets

Seventeen places form a starter shooting profile: her hometown and research connections, landscapes she has photographed, and a few clearly labeled suggestions. The list draws on public biographies, interviews and posts. It is an editorial proposal; Jessica has not confirmed it as her current wishlist.

Priorities run from 1 to 5 and are starting weights, not measured preferences. Coordinates mark broad cities and landscapes. Each entry explains the connection and a possible composition.

Caribou, Maine — hometown

Her publicly documented hometown; northern Maine is central to her early connection with nature. [1] [2]

Shooting idea. Frame the Aroostook landscape around Caribou: forest and agricultural patterns. City-scale subject; no home or family address.

Approximate landscape anchor: 46.861°, -68.012°. Priority 5/5. Documented connection.

Nile and Cairo — night photography

She has repeatedly praised Nile city lights; her May 2026 caption calls the Nile/Cairo/delta sequence a favorite nighttime scene. [13] [14]

Shooting idea. Use Cairo as an anchor for the wider illuminated Nile corridor and delta. Nighttime/timelapse candidate; generic daylight target ranking may undervalue it.

Approximate landscape anchor: 30.044°, 31.236°. Priority 5/5. Publicly expressed interest.

Richat Structure — Earth art

Her Aug 2026 post praises the lighting and color palette of her Richat photograph. [15] [16]

Shooting idea. Fit the concentric rings and surrounding desert into the frame. Favor texture-revealing oblique sunlight; the whole structure is the subject.

Approximate landscape anchor: 21.110°, -11.378°. Priority 5/5. Publicly expressed interest.

La Jolla and Scripps coast

Scripps is her doctoral institution, and she has publicly emphasized the appeal of observing the ocean from orbit. [4]

Shooting idea. Use the La Jolla coastline and Scripps Pier vicinity as the anchor. Wide coastal scene first; the pier is a locator, not a promise of resolved detail.

Approximate landscape anchor: 32.867°, -117.256°. Priority 5/5. Documented connection.

Providence — Brown University

Brown 1999 alumna; in her 2024 address she emphasized the lasting importance of relationships formed there. [3]

Shooting idea. Frame Providence, waterways and the College Hill setting. City-center anchor rather than an individual campus building.

Approximate landscape anchor: 41.824°, -71.413°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Vancouver and UBC peninsula

She led bar-headed-goose flight-physiology research at UBC. [6]

Shooting idea. Use the UBC/Point Grey peninsula in a larger Vancouver, coast and mountain composition. Clear weather is necessary; low cloud can obscure the city.

Approximate landscape anchor: 49.258°, -123.243°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Boston — MGH research connection

She held a Harvard Medical School/Massachusetts General Hospital research appointment before astronaut training. [2]

Shooting idea. Frame the Charles River, Boston and harbor. MGH is a public institutional locator, not an attempt to show a lab interior.

Approximate landscape anchor: 42.363°, -71.069°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Strasbourg — International Space University

Her ISU testimonial explicitly credits her year in Strasbourg and its interdisciplinary education. [7]

Shooting idea. Frame Strasbourg and the Rhine landscape. This is a city-scale anchor; it does not assert a particular favorite monument.

Approximate landscape anchor: 48.585°, 7.751°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Año Nuevo — elephant seal coast

UCSD Earthguide credits her photograph of an Año Nuevo juvenile elephant seal alongside her research contributions. [8] [9]

Shooting idea. Frame Año Nuevo Point, island, beaches and adjacent ocean. Photograph habitat geometry from orbit; individual seals are not realistic subjects.

Approximate landscape anchor: 37.108°, -122.337°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Conch Reef / Key Largo — NEEMO

She served on NEEMO 4 in the Aquarius habitat; Conch Reef is the surrounding public marine-research landscape. [1] [25] [24]

Shooting idea. Look for reef edges, shallows and Keys geometry in daylight. The underwater habitat itself is not visible from ISS.

Approximate landscape anchor: 24.983°, -80.416°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Supramonte, Sardinia — CAVES

Her first-person ESA blog describes Sardinia CAVES 2016; ESA locates Sa Grutta in the Supramonte cave region. [10] [11] [12]

Shooting idea. Frame the rugged eastern Sardinian karst terrain with coastline context. Surface landscape only; the cave is underground.

Approximate landscape anchor: 40.193°, 9.461°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Onilahy mouth, Madagascar — photographed

NASA Johnson credits her Aug 28, 2026 photograph of the Onilahy/Fiherenana coastal area. [17]

Shooting idea. Frame the Onilahy mouth near Saint-Augustin and broader southwest Madagascar coast. Sediment and sandbar shape vary; use current conditions.

Approximate landscape anchor: -23.552°, 43.761°. Priority 4/5. Published photograph.

Salton Sea — photographed

UCSD’s Feb 2026 interview includes her credited photograph of Baja California and the Salton Sea. [5]

Shooting idea. The lake, agricultural geometry and surrounding desert offer strong contrasts. Wider views can connect it to the northern Gulf of California.

Approximate landscape anchor: 33.317°, -115.786°. Priority 4/5. Published photograph.

Black Point Lava Flow — Desert RATS

NASA documents her 2022 simulated-moonwalk work at Black Point Lava Flow. [21] [22]

Shooting idea. Frame the dark lava flow against the pale surrounding landscape and Little Colorado River. Approximate flow-area center, not a historic camp.

Approximate landscape anchor: 35.690°, -111.370°. Priority 4/5. Documented connection.

Sossusvlei dunes — suggested

Suggested from her stated enjoyment of Africa daytime passes; no verified statement identifies Sossusvlei as a personal favorite. [18] [19] [20]

Shooting idea. Frame dune patterns and pale Tsauchab pans. Lower-angle sunlight can separate dune forms; select a wider pattern instead of a single tiny dune.

Approximate landscape anchor: -24.740°, 15.292°. Priority 3/5. Suggested from her interests.

Lighthouse Reef, Belize — suggested

Her Belize diving expeditions are documented; this particular reef is a photographic recommendation, not a verified expedition site or favorite. [1] [23]

Shooting idea. Prioritize the entire atoll and reef edge. The small Great Blue Hole is an optional detailed subject, dependent on clear skies and suitable view.

Approximate landscape anchor: 17.300°, -87.550°. Priority 3/5. Suggested from her interests.

Big Bend, Texas — stated travel interest

In a 2019 NASA interview she explicitly said Big Bend was on her travel list; no visit or current wishlist is established. [28]

Shooting idea. Frame the Big Bend landscape and Rio Grande curves around the Chisos region. This translates a historical travel interest into a photography suggestion.

Approximate landscape anchor: 29.250°, -103.250°. Priority 4/5. Publicly expressed interest.

Viewing constraints and optional places

The ISS ground track reaches about 51.6° north and south. Places beyond that latitude require oblique views; they are not automatically invisible. Weather, sunlight, station attitude, window access and the current orbit determine whether a useful photograph is possible. NASA orbit guidance.

Stockholm — oblique view only. She studied at Stockholm University and chose its flag for her first ISS mission. [26] No direct overhead pass. Attempt a broad oblique city/archipelago scene when geometry, clouds and window view allow. These optional targets have not been added to the starter list.

Västerås — oblique view only. The Swedish Space Agency publicly documents the city as her mother’s hometown. [27] No direct overhead pass. Treat city and Lake Mälaren setting as an optional broad oblique scene; no family address. These optional targets have not been added to the starter list.

McMurdo Sound. Her Antarctic research is relevant background, but McMurdo is too far south for an ISS surface photograph at ordinary station altitude. It is excluded from the shooting list. NASA interview; NSF location.
